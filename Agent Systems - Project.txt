1 Environment Topology
The environment will be represented as a directed scale-free social graph in which nodes
correspond to agents and edges represent relationships between them. Information propagates
between nodes along these edges.
The graph will be generated using the Barabási–Albert model, a network generation model
based on the mechanism of preferential attachment, in which new nodes are more likely to
connect to already well-connected nodes. As a result, the model produces scale-free networks
characterized by a power-law degree distribution, where a small number of nodes called hubs
have a very high number of connections, while the majority of nodes have relatively few.
Consequently, the Barabási–Albert model realistically reflects the structure of social media
networks and, due to the presence of hubs, enables the modeling of the Influencers’ role
described in the system, which corresponds to high-degree nodes with a disproportionately
large reach.
2 Agent Classification
The system will be based on a heterogeneous set of agents representing users of a social media
network. The classification will include the following categories:
• Regular Users: Basic agents of the network who represent the majority of the population. They react to incoming information based on their individual parameter configuration and simultaneously contribute to information diffusion by forwarding or sharing
received content within their local network connections, without playing a specialized
role in the system. Regular Users can transition between all three states of the model:
Susceptible, Believer, and Informed.
• Influencers: Agents characterized by a significantly higher number of connections
within the network (high-degree nodes in the interaction graph), which grants them
a disproportionately large reach in information diffusion. Their behavior is governed
by the same set of parameters as Regular Users, but due to their structural position,
they can disseminate information to a large number of agents, thereby accelerating the
propagation process. Influencers can transition between all three states of the model:
Susceptible, Believer, and Informed.
• Fact-Checkers: Agents responsible for reducing the spread of misinformation by verifying and debunking false content. They interact with other agents by providing
corrective information, which increases the probability of transitioning from Believer to
Informed and directly from Susceptible to Informed. Fact-Checkers operate exclusively
in the Informed state and do not transition between states.
• Bots: Agents responsible for amplifying the spread of misinformation by automatically
generating and disseminating misleading content. They interact with other agents by
providing false information, which increases the probability of transitioning from Susceptible to Believer and raises the Belief value within Believers. Bots operate exclusively
in the Believer state and do not transition between states.

3 Agent States
The dynamics of information spread in the system will be based on a state model implementing an epidemiological approach (SIR). At any point in the simulation, an agent will be in
one of the following states, which determines its susceptibility to external influence as well as
the nature of further information propagation. The model will include the following states:
• Susceptible: The initial state where an agent is unaware of the specific piece of
misinformation but is open to transition to both states.
• Believer: A state in which the agent has accepted the misinformation as true. Agents
in this state act as active spreaders (Infectious), disseminating the fake news to their
neighbors. They remain in this state until they either verify the information themselves
or are convinced by neighboring Informed agents, or until they lose interest (Forgetting
Probability parameter), returning to the Susceptible state.
• Informed: A state in which the agent has recognized the information as false. Agents
in this state act as active Fact-Checkers, spreading corrective information to their
neighbors. They remain in this state until they lose interest (Forgetting Probability
parameter), returning to the Susceptible state.

4 Agent Parameters
Each agent has its own set of parameters, drawn from statistical distributions to reflect
diversity. These parameters govern both cognitive processes and interaction dynamics during
the simulation. The agents are characterized by the following parameters:
1. Belief: A continuous variable representing the agent’s degree of conviction regarding the information. It fluctuates dynamically based on social influence – increasing
through contact with Believers and decreasing when interacting with Fact-Checkers
and Informed agents, or through independent verification. When Belief value crosses
specific thresholds, an agent transitions between states.
2. Competence: A continuous variable representing an agent’s ability to critically analyze information. Higher Competence reduces the probability of transitioning from
Susceptible to Believer.
3. Stubbornness: A continuous variable determining an agent’s resistance to changing their opinion under the influence of neighbors. Higher Stubbornness reduces the
probability of state change.
4. Fact-Checking Probability: A probability value determining the likelihood that an
agent verifies information independently or recognizes it as false after interacting with
Fact-Checkers or Informed agents during a simulation step. Unlike Competence, which
acts as a barrier to infection, this parameter determines how quickly an already infected
agent (Believer) transitions to the Informed state.
5. Forgetting Probability: A probability value representing how likely an agent is to
lose interest in or forget a piece of information over time. This mechanism allows an
agent to return to the Susceptible state over time (reflecting the natural decay of news
relevance and enabling the model to simulate recurring cycles of misinformation).
6. Engagement Level: A continuous variable describing an agent’s activity level and
frequency of participation in the network. Higher Engagement Level increases the
frequency of interactions, leading to faster information diffusion and a higher probability
of state transitions.
7. Trust Level: A set of continuous variables representing the degree of credibility an
agent assigns to different categories of information sources (Regular Users, Influencers,
Fact-Checkers or Bots). Higher Trust Level in a given source increases the influence of
its messages on the agent’s Belief value.
8. Homophily Threshold: A parameter controlling an agent’s tendency to interact
with neighbors who hold similar beliefs. If the difference between agents’ Belief values
exceeds this threshold, the interaction does not occur. Higher Homophily Threshold
promotes the formation of clusters and information bubbles.

5 Interaction Mechanisms
The simulation begins with the generation of a social network based on the Barabási–Albert
model. All Regular Users and Influencers agents are initially assigned to the Susceptible
state, indicating that they have not yet encountered the specific piece of misinformation. All
Bots are assigned to the Believer state, and all Fact-checkers are assigned to the Informed
state.
The system evolves in discrete time steps. During each iteration, all agents are sequentially activated, and their behavior is determined by their type, current state, and individual
parameter values.
At each time step, Bots may generate false information with a predefined probability. This
information is transmitted to their neighboring agents within the network. Simultaneously,
Fact-Checkers actively attempt to counter misinformation by interacting with neighboring
agents and reducing their Belief values with a certain probability.
For Susceptible agents that have already been exposed to information, there exists a
probability of sharing this information with their neighbors. The likelihood of transmission
depends on the agent’s Engagement Level and other behavioral parameters. Agents in the
Believer state act as active spreaders of misinformation, whereas agents in the Informed state
do not propagate false content but instead disseminate corrective information that reduces
the belief value of other agents.
During interactions, agents influence each other’s Belief values. Contact with Bots or
Believers increases the Belief value, whereas interaction with Fact-Checkers or Informed
agents decreases it. These updates are stochastic and modulated by parameters such as
Trust Level, Competence, and Stubbornness.
State transitions are determined by threshold values of the Belief parameter:
• When the Belief exceeds an upper threshold, an agent transitions from Susceptible to
Believer, becoming an active spreader of misinformation.
• When the Belief falls below a lower threshold, an agent transitions to the Informed
state, recognizing the information as false.
Additionally, reverse transitions are possible. For example, a Believer may become Informed due to successful fact-checking, while an Informed agent may revert to the Susceptible
state due to the Forgetting Probability, reflecting the natural decay of attention over time.
Agents in the Susceptible state may share information with a certain probability, whereas
Believers consistently propagate misinformation once infected. This dynamic leads to the
emergence of complex diffusion patterns driven by both local interactions and global network
structure.

6 Technology Stack
We plan to use the following tools in the project:
• Python 3.12: Python will serve as the primary programming language due to its
simplicity and rich ecosystem for data analysis and simulation libraries.
• NetworkX: A Python package for the creation, manipulation, and study of the structure, dynamics, and functions of complex networks. NetworkX will be used to represent
the social network through which information spreads. It allows us to simulate how
fake news propagates through the network and analyze how network structure affects
the speed and reach of misinformation.
• Mesa: An agent-based modeling framework in Python. Mesa will be used to implement
and visualize the simulation. Using its built-in components will allow us to speed up
the development process.
• Matplotlib: A library for creating static, animated, and interactive visualizations in
Python. Matplotlib will be used to visualize the results of the simulation. For example,
we can plot the number of misinformed vs. informed agents over time, and compare
different scenarios.
